# Project Brief Summary

Source: original brief PDF, `Bridge_project_brief.pdf` (full original retained in `report/` for reference — see note at bottom).

## Task

Design and build a 3D wood bridge in a team of 3–4. The bridge model must be a working truss structure, hand-calculated, physically built from a fixed kit of parts, and load-tested to destruction (or to a minimum pass load).

## Geometric constraints

- **Span:** must cross 400 mm between two rigid "cliff" supports.
- **Clearance:** a shipping channel 250 mm wide × 350 mm tall (measured from the high-tide water line) must remain completely clear under the central span — the structure must not intrude into this zone *even under load* (i.e. deflection counts).
- **Supports:** cliffs are 380 mm above the water line. Bridge ends may rest on top of the cliffs, push against them, or hook under a lip ("projection A").
- **Roadway:** must be continuous along the full length, no steps, minimum 60 mm wide, and must clear a 60 mm wide × 70 mm tall "lorry" profile along the full length.
- **Helicopter pad:** a 50×50 mm clear area must be accessible from directly above, centred on the bridge, sitting on the roadway.
- **Loading hole:** a 15 mm diameter hole through the roadway at the bridge centre, for the test rig's loading apparatus.
- **Minimum truss height:** 70 mm (full-scale dimension; see note on units below).
- **Minimum roadway width:** 60 mm.

## Materials (fixed kit, identical for every team)

- 2× balsa strip, 915×6×6 mm
- 2× balsa strip, 915×6×3 mm
- 4× balsa strip, 915×3×3 mm
- 1× "energy board" sheet, 520×90 mm (used as the road deck)
- ~4.3 m of cord, 200 N breaking strength, elastic modulus unspecified
- 1 tube of glue, 1 knife

No other materials permitted.

## Testing and scoring

- Every bridge is physically load-tested on a standard rig.
- **Performance factor, f = Load / Mass.** Teams are ranked by f.
  - Highest f → 100%
  - Lowest f that still clears the 60 N minimum load → 50%
  - Any bridge that fails *below* 60 N is capped at **49%**, regardless of f, and the grade is then entirely down to report quality.
- Every unmet brief criterion (dimensions, clearances, roadway continuity, etc.) costs 10 marks from the initial grade.
- Report quality can move the grade up or down from the initial f-based ranking.

## Required report structure

1. Introduction — background on trusses and bridges
2. Concept design — design options considered, with sketches/diagrams
3. Sketch of the final design
4. Calculations for the final design (force in the most heavily loaded members)
5. Conclusions
6. References

---

### Note on scale

The brief is written with two unit conventions in places (e.g. "70 mm" minimum height alongside hand-drawn calculation sketches dimensioned in cm, like "7 cm"). Our working model used centimetre dimensions consistent with the minimum **70 mm** truss height and **420 mm** span (slightly over the 400 mm minimum, for support clearance) — i.e. our "7 cm" is the brief's "70 mm." This is flagged here for clarity since it could otherwise look like an inconsistency between the brief and the build.
